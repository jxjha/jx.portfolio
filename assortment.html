◆ A note on the data

This case study reflects real work I did on assortment management at Bosch Rexroth. The original figures are confidential and cannot be shown for data-privacy reasons. So I rebuilt the entire analytical approach on a synthetic "drone enterprise" dataset — same schema logic, same business questions, same dashboards — using data I generated myself. Every number you see here is fictional; the thinking is real.

01

Why it matters

Assortment management is the quiet lever behind a hardware business: decide which products to actively stock, promote and price — for each region — and margin follows.

At HQ, those decisions were being made on manual Excel reporting stitched together from a live SAP backend. It was slow, error-prone, and blind to patterns — nobody could quickly answer "which series actually earns its shelf space in the Netherlands?" or "is our discounting in one market quietly eroding margin?"

The goal was to replace guesswork with a queryable, visual system: a clean data model, a library of the questions the business keeps asking, and dashboards that answer them in seconds.
