01

How bias gets in

Bias rarely arrives as malice. It seeps in through four ordinary doors — and a model will happily walk through all of them.

DOOR 01

Skewed data

If the past under-represents a group, the model learns that group as an edge case — or not at all.

DOOR 02

Biased labels

Ground truth is human judgement. "Good hire" or "risky borrower" carries the prejudices of whoever labelled it.

DOOR 03

Proxy features

Drop race or gender and the model finds a stand-in — a postcode, a name, a school — that encodes it anyway.

DOOR 04

Feedback loops

Act on a biased prediction and you generate biased new data. The model confirms its own worldview.

02

Where it bites

The same mechanism, four high-stakes arenas — each one a place where a wrong call changes a life.

Hiring

Résumé screening

Models trained on who got hired before learn to prefer who looked like past hires — penalising gaps, names, and women's colleges.

Credit

Loan & risk scoring

Proxy variables reconstruct protected attributes, producing systematically higher rates or refusals for some communities.

Vision

Facial recognition

Error rates spike for darker skin tones and women when training sets skew light and male — with real consequences in policing.

Speech

Content moderation

Dialect and reclaimed language get flagged as toxic; the people a system should protect are the ones it silences.

04

What to do

Fairness isn't a switch you flip at the end — it's a discipline across the whole lifecycle.

PRE-PROCESS

Fix the data

Re-balance, re-weight, audit labels, and remove leaky proxies before a model ever trains.

IN-PROCESS

Constrain the model

Train with fairness constraints or adversarial de-biasing so equity is an objective, not an afterthought.

POST-PROCESS

Adjust & govern

Calibrate thresholds per group, monitor drift, document with model cards, and keep a human in the loop.

No single metric captures fairness — demographic parity, equal opportunity and calibration can't all hold at once. The real work is choosing, out loud, which definition matters for this decision — and being accountable for it.

Fair by
design.

Bias is a systems problem, not a bug. The tools exist — what's usually missing is the decision to look.
