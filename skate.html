01

The brief

One of the earliest full-time hires, sitting next to the founder at the inflection between "does this work?" and "can we scale it?"

The company had product-market fit on a couple of campuses and was doing three hard things at once: growing to new cities, raising a seed round, and building the operations to support both. The mandate was undefined by design — whatever the business most needed that week became the job.

02

Four hats

03

Making the product decisions honest

Early-stage teams run on the founder's gut. My job was to get the roadmap onto evidence — without killing the pace.

A small, boring toolkit changed how prioritisation felt: Jobs-to-be-Done for why users activated or didn't, cohort analysis for whether launches actually retained, funnel analysis for the drop-offs top-line metrics hide, and PRDs with acceptance criteria so features shipped with the intent they started with.

The discipline the team trusted most: A/B tests before rollout — cheap experiments that killed bad ideas early and backed good ones with data.

04

Ops · fleet uptime as a promise

A shared-scooter business breaks the second a rider walks up to a dead unit. Uptime is not an ops KPI — it's the product itself.

Field metric · sustained across all active campuses

What 95%+ fleet uptime actually took.

95% UPTIME

Uptime is a system, not a heroic effort. Three things did most of the work.

  • Structured maintenance: scheduled by usage-band, not by calendar. High-cycle scooters serviced more often, period.
  • Hardware-feedback loop: a lightweight capture format so every recurring failure pattern reached engineering — not just the loudest one.
  • Weekly ops review: KPIs and OKRs on one page. If a campus dipped below the line, we knew before the users did.
  • Async standups + clear ownership: multi-city with no formal PM infrastructure meant systems had to be lightweight enough to actually get used.

Launch playbook · new campuses

Each campus was a mini product launch — its own research, negotiation, campaign and retrospective.

01
Market read

Sizing, competitor scan, campus-specific regulatory picture. Not every institution is the same.

02
Partnership

Deal terms with campus administrations. Compliance and SOP localisation for that institution.

03
Deployment

Physical logistics of scooter fleet placement. Charging network. Signage. Ground team briefing.

04
Activation

Onboarding campaign timed with the campus academic calendar. Defined activation KPIs.

05
Retro

What worked, what didn't, what the next launch inherits. The playbook improved every time.

Indian university campuses vary wildly in regulatory posture, cultural norms and student behaviour. A one-size-fits-all GTM approach simply doesn't work — the discipline was staying consistent on the process while being fluent on the specifics.

The materials were used in active investor conversations — my first real exposure to how early-stage ventures structure their narratives for capital.
