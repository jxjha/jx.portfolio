The brief

This was my way in — the first role inside Bosch Rexroth and inside German corporate life. The Sales & Distribution team was mid-digitisation, and the internship was written for a generalist: three workstreams, one desk.

HQ needed to modernise three things at once — how it trained a global distributor network, how it surfaced product data for sales decisions, and how its own internal tools felt to use. Rather than hire three specialists, the role bet on one motivated generalist sitting across all three. That suited exactly the mix I'd brought from the Master's: technical curiosity, a design eye, and enough data fluency to be dangerous.

Learning content

Training a global distributor network — video, learning paths, and the pipeline to produce them at scale.

Sales data-viz

Real-time Power BI on a live SAP backend — the work that later grew into the Assortment case study.

UX of internal tools

Turning stakeholder friction into wireframes, flows and specs the dev team could actually build.

02

The AI video pipeline

My most significant build: a production line for AI-generated training video that let one person produce multilingual content — no native speakers, no physical studio, a fraction of the cost and time.

Interactive · the production line

Script to LMS — click a stage.

I also ran the Green Room — the physical production space — for the human-recorded sessions: lighting, audio, technical setup, and post in Adobe Premiere Pro. That gave me quality control across both pipelines, the AI-generated and the live-recorded, feeding one LMS to a single standard.

03

Seven languages, one standard

Learning paths for the product portfolio — categories, sales tools, workflows — built in seven languages. The hard part was never translation. It was making a distributor in Brazil onboard to the same standard as one in Germany.

Localisation is a learning problem, not a language problem.

Each path had to account for what a regional audience already knew, how they learn, and how to sequence content so the outcome was identical across markets. I used AI tools to accelerate translation and localisation while holding terminology and brand voice consistent — then designed the quality-assurance workflow from scratch, because producing technically accurate content in seven languages had no existing playbook to inherit.
