◆ Full academic title

Development of an International Collaboration Platform Between Headquarter and Country Units on Expectation Exchange Model in B2B.

01

The problem

A global industrial giant runs on the trust between two levels: a central HQ that sets strategy, and dozens of country units that execute it. When that trust breaks down quietly, everyone still shows up to work — they just work past each other.

Symptom 01

Duplicated requests

Country units asked HQ for the same things through different channels because no one owned the loop.

Symptom 02

Unmet expectations

HQ didn't know what countries actually needed. Countries didn't know what HQ could realistically deliver.

Symptom 03

Inconsistent knowledge

Product understanding drifted region by region because knowledge transfer had no shared cadence.

Symptom 04

Growing disconnect

Central strategy and local execution stopped feeling like the same organisation to the people inside it.

02

Research approach

A mixed-methods design — quantitative to map, qualitative to understand, analytical to diagnose. Each method chosen to answer a question the previous one couldn't.

01
Method · quantitative

Structured survey

Distributed to country sales representatives across multiple regions to map the current state of expectation alignment.

  • ◆ Baseline metric on collaboration health
  • ◆ Coverage across regions, not anecdotes
  • ◆ Basis for later stratification
02
Method · qualitative

In-depth interviews

Country Sales Heads and Regional Directors — people at the strategic level who could name systemic issues, not just their symptoms.

  • ◆ Access secured via supervisor's network
  • ◆ Semi-structured protocol
  • ◆ Distinguishing structural from cultural friction
03
Method · analytical

Root-cause analysis

Traced every reported friction back to structural origins — where the process itself was breaking, not who was to blame for it.

  • ◆ 21 discrete process gaps surfaced
  • ◆ Grouped by category, not by symptom
  • ◆ Prioritised for the model design
03

21 process gaps

The diagnosis. The single most comprehensive account of this collaboration problem the team had ever formally received — grouped into four categories that later shaped the model.

21
Structural gaps identified

"The most comprehensive diagnosis of this collaboration challenge the team had ever formally received." — supervisor review

◆ Detailed gap list handed over to Bosch Rexroth SDM · protected under thesis confidentiality
04

The model

A structured framework for how HQ and country units should formally exchange needs, capabilities, and constraints on a regular basis. Not a diagram — a living operating rhythm.

Interactive · Expectation Exchange Model

Four flows. One cadence. Measurable alignment.

◆ Hover or tap a flow

Every flow is a formal loop

Each of the four flows has a defined owner, a cadence, a template, and a KPI. The model is not a picture — it's a rhythm that a real organisation can actually run.

Pillar 01
Roles

Named owners on both sides — no more "someone from HQ" or "the local team."

Pillar 02
Cadences

Regular exchange windows, not ad-hoc requests. Predictable enough to plan around.

Pillar 03
Templates

Shared structure for needs, capabilities and constraints so the signal doesn't get lost in the format.

Pillar 04
KPIs

Measurable indicators of alignment — so improvement is visible, not vibes-based.

05

Implementation roadmap

The model was the diagnosis-plus-cure. The roadmap made it usable — a phased plan mapping exactly which stakeholders needed to do what, in what sequence, to make the exchange operational.

Phase 01

Ground truth

Baseline the current state with the survey data. Publish the 21 gaps as a shared reality across HQ and country leadership.

Phase 02

Structure

Assign named role owners on both sides. Publish the templates and the exchange cadence. Instrument the KPIs.

Phase 03

Operate & iterate

Run the exchange for a full quarter. Review KPI movement with SDM leadership. Tighten templates from lived use, not theory.

The roadmap was stress-tested through alignment sessions with Sales Heads, Product Managers, and the Sales Director before being submitted — so it wasn't just academically defensible, it was operationally credible.

06
