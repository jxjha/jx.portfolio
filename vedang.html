01

A real client, live

Most of the work in this portfolio is mine to define — my questions, my scope, my finish line. This one was different. Vedang Yoga is a real client, and vedangyoga.in is a real website, live on its own domain and serving the practice it was built for.

It's the piece I'm happiest to point at, because someone other than me decided it was good enough to ship. A yoga practice doesn't need a loud website; it needs a calm, trustworthy one — a place that feels like the room the classes are held in, tells a visitor plainly what's on offer, and makes reaching out effortless.

That was the whole brief, and the whole discipline: get out of the way of the practice, and let the site do three quiet jobs well — set the tone, explain the offering, open a door to get in touch.

03

The elements

A yoga site earns trust in the first few seconds, so every part pulls toward calm and clarity. Here's how the pieces are meant to work — the live embed above is the real thing.

Fig. 01 · Landingassets/vedang/shot-hero.png
A calm first impression

The landing sets the tone before it says a word

The first screen does the heavy lifting: warm, unhurried, breathing room around the type. Before a visitor reads anything, they should already feel that this is a considered, gentle place — the digital equivalent of a quiet studio with the mats laid out.

Fig. 02 · Offeringassets/vedang/shot-offering.png
Clarity on what's on offer

What the practice is, in plain language

The middle of the site answers the visitor's real question — is this for me? The offering is laid out without jargon, so a first-timer and a returning student both find their footing quickly. Legible, generously spaced, never a wall of text.

Fig. 03 · Get in touchassets/vedang/shot-contact.png
A door that's easy to open

Reaching out is one tap, not a hunt

The whole point of a small business site is the moment someone decides to make contact. That path is kept short and obvious — the way to enquire or join is always within reach, so interest never has to survive a scavenger hunt to become a message.

Fig. 04 · On a phoneassets/vedang/shot-mobile.png
Built for the phone first

Where most people will actually find it

A local practice gets discovered on a phone, mid-scroll, between other things. So the site is built to feel right there first — tap targets sized for thumbs, type that stays readable at arm's length, and a layout that reflows cleanly instead of shrinking a desktop page.

04

Built with Claude

The interesting part of this one isn't only the site — it's how it got made. I designed and built it with Claude as the pair-programmer, directing the work, making the calls on tone and structure, and shipping a production website to a client's own domain.

Working this way puts the emphasis where it belongs: on taste, direction and judgement. Deciding what "calm" should look like, what to leave out, how a first-time visitor should feel — those are the calls that make or break a small-business site, and they're mine. The AI is the fast hands; the intent is human.

And it's not a sketch on a laptop. It went the whole distance — designed, built, put on a domain, and handed over live. That end-to-end delivery, for someone who was counting on it, is the part I'd want a client to know I can do.
